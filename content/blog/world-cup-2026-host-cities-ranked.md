## The Ranking Criteria

This ranking is for fans — not pundits, not journalists, not FIFA officials. The criteria:

1. **Match-day atmosphere** (crowd passion, stadium environment)
2. **Fan experience beyond the stadium** (fan hubs, sports bars, street culture)
3. **Tourism value** (things to do between matches)
4. **Logistics** (how easy is it to get around, get to the stadium, get between cities)
5. **Value for money** (affordability of accommodation, food, and transport)

With that in mind:

## Tier 1: Don't Miss

### 1. Mexico City

No contest for the top spot. The Azteca on June 11 will be the most emotionally charged sporting event in the world — a nation hosting the World Cup for the third time, with a team now good enough to go deep.

The city itself is extraordinary — 22 million people, world-class museums, cuisine that's been called the best in the Americas, a nightlife scene that starts at midnight and ends at dawn, and a football culture that permeates everything.

The fan hub at the Zócalo alone would justify the trip.

**Challenge:** Altitude (2,240m) requires acclimatization. Traffic is world-class terrible.

### 2. New York / New Jersey

The final. MetLife Stadium on July 19 for the World Cup final. The most iconic city in the world. If you're going to any one match at this tournament, make it the final in New York.

Beyond the final, New York has unmatched football community culture — every single national team has a large, passionate diaspora in the five boroughs, and the city knows how to do a massive event.

**Challenge:** The most expensive host city. Book everything as far in advance as possible.

### 3. Kansas City

The sleeper pick of the tournament. Arrowhead Stadium is genuinely the loudest stadium in the world — the noise on a match day will be unlike anything most fans have experienced. The tailgate culture is extraordinary. Kansas City BBQ is world-class and cheap. The city is genuinely affordable.

Fans who've been to an NFL game at Arrowhead know what's coming. Everyone else will be blown away.

**Challenge:** No public transit to the stadium. Rideshare or shuttle only.

## Tier 2: Excellent

### 4. Houston

The most culturally diverse large city in the USA produces the most authentic international football fan culture. Mexican, Central American, Nigerian, Colombian — every major footballing nation has a massive, passionate community here. Matches at NRG Stadium will feel more like home games for multiple nations simultaneously.

**Challenge:** June–July heat is serious. The retractable roof controls stadium conditions but outdoor queues are brutal.

### 5. Los Angeles

LA's football scene has genuinely arrived — LAFC and the Galaxy have built a real supporter culture, the city's Latin American community is enormous, and SoFi Stadium is a spectacular venue. The Metro K Line connection to the stadium is excellent.

The tourism case doesn't need making — it's Los Angeles.

**Challenge:** Sprawl. Getting between LA neighborhoods requires planning.

### 6. Vancouver

BC Place is stunning. The mountains behind the stadium are stunning. The SkyTrain to the stadium is the best stadium transit of any host city. Vancouver in July is beautiful.

Canada's home matches here will be electric — the city's Portuguese and Central American communities are passionate, and Canada's national team has genuine support for the first time in a generation.

**Challenge:** Most expensive Canadian city. Book accommodation early.

## Tier 3: Great

### 7. Miami

The spectacle and glamour of Miami in the summer is exactly what a World Cup should feel like. The stadium experience at Hard Rock is great, the city is beautiful, and the Cuban, Colombian, and Brazilian communities make South Florida feel like a second home for half the tournament's teams.

**Challenge:** Heat, traffic, and prices.

### 8. Guadalajara

The spiritual home of Mexican football culture is underrated by international fans. Estadio Akron is architecturally spectacular, the city is beautiful, the food is extraordinary, and the Chivas fanbase creates some of the most passionate atmospheres in all of Mexico.

Tequila and mariachi are not clichés here — they're genuinely integral to the culture.

**Challenge:** Less accessible for international visitors than Mexico City.

### 9. Seattle

Lumen Field is one of the best stadiums in MLS — the home of the Sounders has trained some of the most sophisticated supporter culture in American football. The city is beautiful, the food is excellent (salmon, coffee, craft beer), and the transit to the stadium is effortless.

**Challenge:** Weather is variable even in July. Pacific Northwest rain is real.

### 10. Atlanta

MARTA to the stadium is the best transit of any US host city (tied with Seattle). Mercedes-Benz Stadium is genuinely world-class. The food scene is outstanding. The city's African and Caribbean communities bring real football passion.

**Challenge:** Hot and humid in July.

## Tier 4: Good but Specialist Interest

### 11. Toronto

BMO Field has a beautiful waterfront setting and the 509/511 streetcar connection to Union Station is easy. Toronto's Portuguese community will make certain matches extraordinary.

**Challenge:** BMO Field is smaller than many other venues (30,000 capacity). The city is expensive.

### 12. Dallas

Great value, accessible city, AT&T Stadium is impressive, and the DFW area is easy to navigate. Strong BBQ and Tex-Mex food culture.

**Challenge:** No public transit to the stadium — rideshare only.

### 13. Monterrey

The most visually dramatic stadium setting in the tournament. Estadio BBVA with the Cerro de la Silla mountain behind it is breathtaking. Rayados fans are exceptional.

**Challenge:** The heat in June is genuinely extreme. Less familiar to international visitors.

### 14. Philadelphia

Lincoln Financial Field is in a great stadium complex with easy SEPTA access. Philadelphia is an underrated food city (cheesesteaks, Italian market, Reading Terminal Market). The city has real character.

**Challenge:** Less glamorous reputation than other East Coast hosts.

### 15. San Francisco Bay Area

Levi's Stadium in Santa Clara is a good venue but the logistics are complex — three airports, Caltrain connections, and the Bay Area's traffic is legendary. The tourism case for San Francisco is outstanding; the stadium experience requires planning.

**Challenge:** The most logistically complex US host city.

### 16. Boston

Gillette Stadium is good. The commuter rail connection to Foxborough is manageable (if you book your return ticket before kickoff). Boston is beautiful and walkable.

**Challenge:** 40km from the city to the stadium is the longest of any host city, and the commuter rail is the only public transit option. It can sell out.

---

No bad choices here — every host city will have something genuinely special to offer. But if you're building your itinerary, prioritize Tier 1 and 2 cities, and use our individual [city guides](/) to plan the details.
