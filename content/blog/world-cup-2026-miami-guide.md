## Miami for the 2026 World Cup

Miami is one of the most cosmopolitan cities in the Western Hemisphere. The Cuban, Colombian, Venezuelan, Brazilian, and Haitian communities make South Florida feel like a second home for a huge portion of the tournament's teams. Match days in Miami won't feel like neutral-site events — they'll feel like home games for whoever's playing.

Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens hosts several group stage matches and potentially knockout rounds. Here's everything you need.

## Hard Rock Stadium

Hard Rock Stadium is a retractable-roof venue that holds approximately 65,000 fans. The roof is critical — Miami in June and July is brutally hot and humid, and the stadium's climate control makes it genuinely comfortable for an afternoon match.

**Address:** 347 Don Shula Drive, Miami Gardens, FL 33056

The stadium is in Miami Gardens, about 18 miles north of Downtown Miami and 15 miles north of Miami Beach.

## Getting to Hard Rock Stadium

**By Uber/Lyft:** The most common option. From Downtown Miami, budget 30–45 minutes pre-match, and potentially 60–90 minutes post-match (traffic). Drop-off is designated near the stadium.

**By shuttle:** The official FIFA shuttle network will operate from Downtown Miami and Miami Beach. Check the official tournament site for routes and schedules.

**By car:** Parking is available on-site at $50–100+ on match days. Exit after matches is slow — budget 45–60 minutes to clear the lot. The Turnpike/I-95 corridor backs up significantly.

**By Tri-Rail:** The South Florida commuter rail has a station at Opa-locka (2.5 miles from the stadium). Shuttle service will connect Opa-locka station to the stadium during matches. The Tri-Rail runs between Miami Airport and West Palm Beach — useful for fans staying north.

## Neighborhoods to Stay In

**South Beach (Miami Beach):** The glamour pick. 15–20 miles from the stadium but the hotels and nightlife are unmatched. Take a shuttle to avoid the drive. Book early — this will be the most in-demand area.

**Brickell:** Miami's financial district has excellent modern hotels within 15 minutes of Downtown Miami. Quieter than South Beach, better access to Metrorail.

**Downtown Miami:** Central location, Metrorail access, mid-range hotel options. More practical than South Beach for fans prioritizing logistics over the beach experience.

**Wynwood:** The art and nightlife district has boutique hotels and immediate access to Miami's best bars. 20 minutes from the stadium.

**Doral:** Close to Miami Airport and 15 minutes from Hard Rock Stadium. Good value hotels and easy access. Lower on atmosphere but practical.

## Best Bars to Watch Games in Miami

**Ball & Chain** (Little Havana) — Iconic Calle Ocho bar with live Latin music, salsa dancing, and an atmosphere that perfectly captures Miami's Cuban cultural heart. Mexican and South American matches will be extraordinary here.

**Bar Nancy** (Little Haiti) — Neighborhood bar with genuine football passion, great rum drinks, and the Caribbean energy that only Miami can produce.

**Kiki on the River** (Overtown) — Rooftop and waterfront views, usually with a soccer-watching setup for big events. The setting is spectacular for evening matches.

**Fado Irish Pub** (Brickell) — Reliable FIFA 2026 official broadcast, proper pint, and a football-literate international crowd. Opens early for morning matches.

**American Social Bar** (Brickell) — Large venue with multiple screens, good food, and handles big game crowds well.

**The Wharf Miami** (Downtown) — Outdoor waterfront beer garden with food trucks and multiple screens. Enormous capacity — one of the best for large groups.

## Little Havana: The Cultural Center

For any match involving South American or Caribbean teams, Little Havana — specifically Calle Ocho — will be the epicenter of Miami's World Cup experience. The street parties, the domino parks, the salsa soundtrack, and the passion of the Cuban and Colombian communities make it unlike any other neighborhood in America.

Walk Calle Ocho on a match day involving any Latin American team and you'll understand why Miami is one of the best World Cup cities.

## The Fan Experience

**Heat:** Miami in June–July averages 90°F/32°C with high humidity. The stadium has climate control. Outside the stadium, prepare for the heat — light clothing, sunscreen, hydration.

**Afternoon matches:** The hottest part of the day. The stadium's roof handles it. Getting to and from the stadium is when you'll feel it.

**Evening matches:** Much more pleasant. South Beach and Brickell at night in June are spectacular.

**Language:** Miami is genuinely bilingual. Spanish is everywhere. This makes it uniquely welcoming for Spanish-speaking fans from South America and Europe.

Miami will deliver spectacle, culture, and atmosphere in equal measure. Book early — this is one of the most in-demand cities in the tournament — and choose your base based on priorities: South Beach for the full Miami experience, Brickell or Downtown for logistics.
