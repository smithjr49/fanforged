## New York City: The World Cup Capital of North America

New York City is arguably the best place on the planet to watch the World Cup. MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford will host more matches than any other venue, including the final on July 19. The city's immigrant communities from every football-mad nation on Earth mean every single match has a home crowd somewhere in the five boroughs.

Here's where to be for the best atmospheres.

## Manhattan: The Classics

**Smithfield Bar & Restaurant (Midtown/Hell's Kitchen)** — Multiple screens, consistently the best atmosphere for England and European matches in Manhattan. Arrive 45 minutes early for knockout matches.

**Stitch Bar (Midtown)** — Sports bar that gets a devoted following for major international matches. Known for having every screen on the same match with real volume.

**Banter (East Village)** — Young, lively crowd and a great bar program. One of the best East Village options for watching football.

**The Gaf (Upper East Side / Upper West Side)** — Two locations of this excellent sports bar, both known for soccer. Strong weekend morning following for European fixtures.

## Brooklyn: The Best in the Boroughs

**Football Factory at Legends (Williamsburg)** — Dedicated soccer bar with memorabilia from clubs worldwide. The go-to spot for Williamsburg football culture.

**Bem Brasil (Park Slope/Gowanus area)** — When Brazil plays, nowhere in New York gets louder. The Brazilian community turns out in force.

**Tara Rose (Park Slope)** — Irish pub institution with a real soccer-watching culture and a neighborhood crowd that knows the game.

## Queens: The Real Football Heartland

Queens is where New York's football culture actually lives. The neighborhoods of Jackson Heights (Colombian, Ecuadorian, Mexican communities), Astoria (Greek, Middle Eastern), and Flushing (Korean, Chinese) all have deep football traditions.

**La Flor Bakery & Café (Woodside)** — An Irish-Mexican hybrid in Queens that sounds unlikely but works perfectly. Strong football culture.

**Jackson Diner area (Jackson Heights)** — Not a single bar, but the stretch of Roosevelt Avenue through Jackson Heights turns into an outdoor festival when South American teams play. Just walk the strip.

## New Jersey: Near the Stadium

MetLife Stadium is in East Rutherford, NJ — about 30 minutes from Midtown Manhattan. The Meadowlands area doesn't have much within walking distance, so pre-match is best done in Manhattan or the nearby Hoboken/Jersey City bar scene:

**Mulligan's Pub (Hoboken)** — Closest great soccer bar to the stadium transit corridor. Hoboken PATH station puts you 25 minutes from Penn Station.

**Zeppelin Hall (Jersey City)** — Huge beer garden in Liberty State Park. Perfect for summer match days with outdoor space.

## Morning Match Survival Guide

Many World Cup group stage matches kick off at 9am, 12pm, and 3pm Eastern Time. For early matches:

- Most bars open for 9am kickoffs — call ahead to confirm
- Midtown bars tend to open earlier than neighborhood spots
- Queens spots along Roosevelt Ave often open without fuss for major matches

## Getting to MetLife Stadium

For match days: NJ Transit trains from Penn Station (Midtown) to Meadowlands Station — check the NJ Transit Meadowlands schedule. The journey is about 30-40 minutes and trains run frequently before and after matches.

Do not drive to MetLife on match days unless you arrive 3+ hours early. The parking situation is well-managed but slow.
